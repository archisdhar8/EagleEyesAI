# Fundamentals are not valuation

Fundamentals describe the business. Valuation describes the price paid relative to evidence about that business.

## Separate the questions

Revenue growth, margins, cash generation, debt, and competitive position help assess the company. Earnings multiples, cash-flow yields, comparable companies, and scenario assumptions help assess valuation.

Portfolio fit is another separate question. A strong company at a reasonable valuation can still increase an investor's concentration or duplicate an ETF holding.

## Common misconception

A high-quality company is not automatically an attractive investment at every price, and an apparently cheap company may be cheap because its business evidence is deteriorating.
